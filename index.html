Job market paper

Aiming for AllOptimal Design of Reward in Word-of-Mouth Marketing

Tianzhuang Xu, Boer Ouyang, and Ying-Ju Chen

Referral rewards encourage customer participation but also increase firms’ costs. Our random-graph model links rewards to participation, diffusion, and profits, allowing us to characterize campaign reach and determine the optimal reward. We also use contract theory to examine how firms can implement incentives when customers’ numbers of friends are private information.

Analyzing Network Formation Using Big Data Analytics: A Study of Degree Distribution, Clustering Coefficient, Diameter, and Assortativity

Hao Li, Tianzhuang Xu*, Jin Qi, and Ying-Ju Chen

* Corresponding author

An edge-based search model captures how networks form when people encounter interactions or content before discovering one another. The model generates a scale-free network structure, and we establish exponential convergence toward a stable state. We evaluate its predictions using simulations and real-world data.

When Multi-sourcing Backfires: Competition and Market Robustness in Supply Networks

Tianzhuang Xu, Boer Ouyang, and Ying-Ju Chen

Under review at Production and Operations Management.

Additional sourcing relationships can weaken market robustness when firms respond strategically to disruption risk. In a network game with heterogeneous suppliers, we show that adding links can reduce expected output and worsen expected shortages. Selective connectivity can improve robustness by reallocating production after firms adjust their prices and orders.

Work in progress

Learning Network Formation: Toward Autonomous Consistency

Autonomous consistency concerns whether a learned model preserves the statistical properties of stochastic network growth when repeatedly applied to its own generated networks. Using analytically tractable benchmarks, we investigate whether transformer-based models achieve this consistency beyond the training horizon and how model design and training affect the reproduction of degree distributions, connectivity, and clustering.

The Value of Network Information in Diffusion

How much does knowing exact network connections add to diffusion predictions based on aggregate statistics? Using stochastic diffusion models, we examine when statistical descriptions provide reliable predictions and when detailed structure materially improves them. We also investigate how this value depends on the choice of initial seeds and what information is needed to guide diffusion interventions.

Livestream Selling: Pricing, Presentation, and Buyer Competition

Livestream sellers of inexpensive, one-of-a-kind items must allocate scarce airtime across products. Our dynamic model captures how presentation can build interest while waiting incurs attention costs and the risk of losing an item to another buyer. Buyers can purchase immediately, continue watching, wait for future products, or leave. We examine how posted prices and presentation durations shape purchase timing and revenue per unit of time.
